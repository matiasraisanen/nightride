NIGHTRIDE - USER MANUAL
=======================

A terminal player for Nightride FM synthwave radio (https://nightride.fm/), in
the style of the MS-DOS programs of the early 90s.

This is an unofficial player, not made by Nightride FM. The music and the song
info come from their public streams.

In the player, Help > User manual opens this file.

1. STARTING THE PLAYER
----------------------

Run "nightride" in a terminal. On Windows, you can also double-click
nightride.exe.

  nightride --test-stream [STATION]
      Plays a station for a few seconds without the interface, and says
      whether it worked.

  nightride --debug
      Logs every key press and more, for finding bugs.

  nightride --version
      Shows the version.

The player starts on the station and volume you used last.


2. THE MAIN SCREEN
------------------

The main screen shows the station, the artist and song playing, how long the
station has played, the volume, and the VU meter.

  Left, Right or 1-7    Change station
  Up, Down              Volume in steps of 10
  Shift-Up, Shift-Down  Volume in steps of 1
  M                     Mute
  F2                    Stations
  F3                    Recently played
  F                     Find this song
  V                     VU meter on or off
  C, D                  Clock, date on or off
  T, Shift-T            Next, previous theme
  F10                   Menus, or Alt and the red letter of a menu
  F1                    All the keys
  Esc                   Exit, after asking
  Alt-X, F12            Exit right away

In the menus and dialogs, the arrow keys and Tab move, Enter chooses and Esc
closes.


3. MENUS
--------

Press F10, or Alt and the red letter of a menu, like Alt-R for Radio.

  Radio     Stations, Recently played, Find this song, Mute, Exit
  Options   VU meter, Clock, Date, Mouse, and Settings
  Theme     The colour themes
  Help      Keys, User manual, Stream info, the Nightride.fm website, About

Themes: the highlighted theme shows right away. Enter keeps it, and Esc goes
back to the one you had.

  Synthwave   Purple and cyan on dark grey (the default)
  Outrun      Neon pink, orange and yellow on navy
  Darksynth   Blood red and grey on black
  DOS         The classic blue MS-DOS look
  C64         Light blue on dark blue, like a Commodore 64
  Amber CRT   A single-colour amber monitor
  Green CRT   A single-colour green monitor

Settings has the station and volume to start with (by default the ones you
used last), the clock and date formats, syncing the song info with the audio,
and which messages to show.


4. RECENTLY PLAYED
------------------

F3 lists the last 10 songs on the station playing, newest first, with the time
each one started. Number 1 is the song playing now.

Pick a song with the arrow keys or its number (0 for the 10th), and press
Enter or F to find it.

The list covers your current listening session, also past midnight. It is kept
when you restart the player, and starts afresh after two hours without song
info: when the player has been closed, or the computer asleep or offline.

Station jingles are left out of the list. Live shows are in it, marked "Live".


5. FIND THIS SONG
-----------------

F, or clicking the artist or song, asks where to look for the song:

  Bandcamp   Buying the track on Bandcamp is the best way to support the
             artist, so it comes first.
  Spotify    Opens the song itself when the station's stream says which song
             it is on Spotify, which it does for about half the songs.
             Otherwise it searches Spotify.
  YouTube    Searches YouTube.
  Google     Searches Google, with the station's name added.

Press the first letter of a site (B, S, Y, G), or move to it and press Enter.
It opens in your web browser.


6. JINGLES AND LIVE SHOWS
-------------------------

Between songs, Nightride FM plays short jingles read by artists, like "Jingle
by Retrax". They show with a JINGLE label next to the song, and aren't songs
to find, so F says so instead.

Live shows show with a LIVE label, and as "Live: <show>" in the messages.


7. MOUSE
--------

- Click the menus, the buttons, and the key hints on the bottom line.
- In Stations and Recently played, click to pick a row, and double-click to
  play the station or find the song.
- On the main screen, click the station to open Stations, and the artist or
  song to find it. Click the volume bar to set the volume, the arrows next to
  it to step by 10, and the number to mute.
- The scroll wheel changes the volume on the main screen, and scrolls the
  lists.

While the player has the mouse, most terminals select text with Shift held
down. Options > Mouse turns the mouse off.


8. MESSAGES
-----------

A message above the bottom line says what a key did, shows each new song, and
tells when something goes wrong. Settings > Notifications chooses which ones
show.

The song and the station are also in the terminal's window title. Some
terminals, like Konsole, only show titles set by programs if their tab title
format includes %w.


9. SONG INFO AND THE AUDIO SYNC
-------------------------------

Nightride FM's song info comes about 16 seconds before you hear the song, so
the player measures the delay and shows each song when it starts in the audio.

The delay is how far the player is behind the live stream, which VLC reports,
plus how long after the song info the song starts in the stream, which the
player checks twice an hour from the song titles in the stream (about 0.7 MB
each time). If it can't measure them, it shows the song info as it comes, says
"Song info is not in sync with the audio", and the log tells why.

Settings > Sync song info turns syncing off.


10. STREAM INFO
---------------

Help > Stream info shows how the stream is doing. It updates while it is open.

  Station         The station playing.
  Address         Where the audio comes from.
  Status          Playing for how long, or Connecting, Reconnecting, Stream
                  error (it keeps trying), No audio output or Stopped.
  Format          What the stream server says it sends: the audio format,
                  bitrate, sample rate, and mono or stereo. It is checked each
                  time the dialog opens.
  Received        The data received since the station started playing, and the
                  bitrate VLC measures. The bitrate shows after a few seconds.
  Dropouts        Trouble VLC has counted: "lost" is audio that came too late
                  to play, heard as a skip, "broken" is data that couldn't be
                  read, and "gaps" are breaks in the stream. "None" is how it
                  should be.
  Reconnects      How many times the stream has dropped and the player
                  connected again, since the station was chosen.
  Behind live     How far the audio you hear is behind the live stream. The
                  server starts each listener with some seconds of past audio.
  Song info feed  Whether the player is connected to Nightride FM's song info,
                  which says what each station is playing. It is a separate
                  connection from the audio, so the music can play while it is
                  down.
  Song info gap   How long after the song info a song starts in the live
                  stream, and when that was measured. "From before" means the
                  value is from an earlier run, and hasn't been measured again
                  yet.
  Sync            How long after the song info each song shows on the screen:
                  Behind live plus Song info gap. See section 9.


11. SETTINGS AND FILES
----------------------

  Linux
    Settings         ~/.config/nightride/settings.ini
    Log              ~/.local/state/nightride/radio.log
    Recently played  ~/.local/state/nightride/history.json

  Windows
    Settings         %APPDATA%\nightride\settings.ini
    Log              %APPDATA%\nightride\radio.log
    Recently played  %APPDATA%\nightride\history.json

Most settings can be changed in the player, but settings.ini can also be
edited by hand while the player is closed:

  default_station       Station to start on, or "last" for the one played last.
  start_volume          Volume to start with, 0 to 99, or "last" for the one
                        used last.
  clock_format,         The clock and date formats in use, as strftime formats
  date_format           (https://strftime.org).
  clock_formats,        The formats Settings cycles through. Add and remove
  date_formats          them freely, one per line, indented. A format that
                        doesn't work is left out, with a message, and the log
                        says why.
  notifications         "all", "off", or any of "songs", "actions" and
                        "errors", separated by commas.
  sync_song_info        True to show each song when it starts in the audio,
                        False when the song info says.
  theme                 One of the theme names.
  vu_meter, clock,      True or False.
  date, mouse
  [STATIONS]            The stations and their number keys.
  [URLS]                The stream and song info addresses, the website, and
                        the sites for Find this song. {query} is replaced with
                        the song, and {id} in spotify_track with its Spotify
                        ID.

The log is kept across restarts, with each start and exit marked. It keeps the
last day, and at most about 1 MB.


12. TROUBLESHOOTING
-------------------

No sound
  "nightride --test-stream" plays a station without the interface, and says
  whether it worked. The log may say more.

A key does nothing
  The terminal may use it for something of its own, like F10 or Esc. Unbind it
  in the terminal's settings, or use the menus.

NO AUDIO OUTPUT
  VLC is playing but can't reach the sound system, for example when PipeWire
  is stuck. This warning only works on Linux for now.

The VU meter says it needs something
  On Linux, it needs parec and pactl, which come with PulseAudio and PipeWire
  (package pulseaudio-utils on Debian and Ubuntu).

Clicks select text in cmd.exe
  The player turns off QuickEdit while it runs, so clicks reach it. If they
  still select text, turn QuickEdit off in the window's properties.

The window is too small
  The player needs at least 50 columns by 14 rows, or 16 with the VU meter on.
  It looks best at 80 by 25, like in the old days.

The colours look off
  The themes show their exact colours in terminals with true colour, like
  Konsole and Windows Terminal. Other terminals get the closest colours they
  have.

About the VU meter
  On Linux, it records the player's own audio from PulseAudio or PipeWire, and
  shows its average level. On Windows, it reads the same meter as the Windows
  volume mixer. That meter gives the loudest samples rather than the average,
  so the player takes 9 dB off them to read like the Linux meter. Both measure
  the music itself, so the volume doesn't change them, but they drop to zero
  while muted.
